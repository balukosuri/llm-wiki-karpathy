### 280 Mini Driver (2025)

| Loft | Lie | Length | SW |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13° | 56.0° | 43.5" | D3 |

Available in LH/RH. A 280cc head bridging the gap between a driver and 3-wood: longer than a 3-wood (43.5" vs ~43"), lower spin and more distance than a fairway wood, more controllable than a 460cc driver. Forged L-Cup Face; Seamless Thermoform Crown; adjustable weighting. Fitting context: players who cannot reliably hit a 460cc driver off the tee or need a reliable tee club on tight holes; also playable from the turf unlike a standard driver. Replaces the 3-wood in some bags. The 56° lie angle reflects its fairway-wood-like delivery.

**Data note:** The GT2 spec database entries at 13°/56°/43.5" overlap with the 280 Mini Driver specs (GT2 shows D4 vs 280's D3). This appears to be a database artifact — the 13° spec under GT2 is likely a duplicate/mislabeled mini driver entry. Treat GT2 as an 8°–11° driver only.

The SM11 standardizes CG placement within each loft for consistent trajectory and feel (a change from SM10's progressive CG philosophy). New directional face texture enhances greenside spin. Grind options include F, K, D, S, M depending on loft.

- **F grind:** Neutral; most versatile; works for most swing types
- **S grind:** Moderate relief; for neutral-to-steep attack angles
- **D grind:** High bounce with heel/toe relief; for steep attack angles, soft conditions
- **K grind:** Low bounce; for shallow/sweeping attack angles, firm conditions
- **M grind:** Medium relief; mid-bounce; all-around for mid-bounce needs

### Market Position

Titleist positions the entire lineup as aspirational/premium — even the T350 (their most forgiving iron) avoids the large-cavity, oversized look of competing GI irons. This matters for fittings: a player who "doesn't want to look like they're playing GI clubs" can often be fit into a T350 without objection, whereas the same player might refuse a TaylorMade Stealth HD or Callaway Paradym AI on aesthetics alone.

The Black Edition models (T100, T150, T200, T350) are cosmetic variants with Titanium Carbide Vapor finish. They carry the same technology as the standard finishes — don't recommend them unless the player specifically wants the darker look.

The GTS series (2026) is clearly positioned as a generational refresh. Limited spec data currently available — confirm with Titleist fitting guides as data becomes available.

## Data Quality Notes

1. **GT2 driver description:** Database description incorrectly says "GT280 Mini Driver" for all GT2 entries. Actual GT2 is a standard driver at 8°–11°. The 13° entry under GT2 with 56°/43.5" specs duplicates the 280 Mini Driver spec (minor SW difference: D4 vs D3) — likely a database artifact; treat GT2 as 8°–11° only.
2. **GT1 driver description:** Marked as "Women's Driver" in DB. Existing [[titleist-gt-metals]] page treats it as a standard high-launch model. Both interpretations hold — GT1 targets moderate SS (applies to women's, seniors, and slower male players).
3. **GT3 fairway:** Suspicious 2W entries at 8°/9° and a 3W at 10° with 58° lie/42" length — likely misclassified data; standard GT3 FW starts at 13.5° 3W.
4. **T250:** "9i" entry at 35° with no lie/length alongside standard "9" at 38.5° — treat 38.5° as correct; 35° entry appears erroneous.
5. **T250 Star:** Two parallel spec sets present (D2 standard and C8 lightweight). C8 lofts are identical to T250 Launch Spec — possibly the same product, or a shaft-only variant. Verify at point of sale.
6. **T350 / T350 Black Edition:** Two "PW" entries at 43° and 48°. The 48° is likely a GW mislabeled as PW. Confirmed separate GW at 53° also in set.
7. **U505 / U•505:** Listed in both hybrids and irons categories in source database with identical specs; same product.
8. **GTS2 / GTS3 drivers and fairways:** Lie, length, and swing weight absent across all entries.
9. **T200 Black:** Swing weight absent from spec database across all clubs.
