Measures the rotational angle of the pelvis around the vertical axis at address, backswing maximum, and impact.

## What It Measures
Tracks pelvis rotation (hip turn) in the face-on view across three swing phases. Negative values indicate the pelvis is turned away from the target (backswing direction); positive values indicate the pelvis is open toward the target. Evaluated at address for alignment, at backswing maximum for coil depth, and at impact for hip clearing.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alignment | ADR | < 3° | Face On |
| Make A Full Turn | BS MAX | < -37° | Face On |
| Get Body More Open | IMP | > 32° | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Pelvis turn is the rotational engine of the golf swing and the leading segment in the kinematic chain. At address, keeping the pelvis square (< 3° open) establishes a neutral base from which both backswing coil and downswing rotation can operate symmetrically. At backswing maximum, a turn of at least 37° into the trail side stores rotational energy and stretches the hip-torso separation angle. At impact, the pelvis must have rotated at least 32° open toward the target — hips clearing through impact drives the distal segments (chest, arms, club) through the ball via the kinematic chain and is the primary generator of clubhead speed.

## Fault Patterns
- **At ADR: too open (> 3°):** Pelvis is pre-rotated toward the target; reduces effective backswing coil and can create alignment issues.
- **At BS MAX: insufficient turn (> -37°, i.e., less than 37°):** Limited hip turn in the backswing constrains energy storage and often forces the upper body to over-rotate to compensate.
- **At IMP: not open enough (< 32°):** Hips have not cleared through impact; the pelvis is blocking, causing the arms and club to stall, which often results in a flip, loss of speed, or blocked/pushed shots.
