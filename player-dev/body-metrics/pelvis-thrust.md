Measures how far the pelvis has thrust toward the ball-target line (toward the camera in the DTL view) at impact.

## What It Measures
Quantifies the forward (toward the ball) translation of the pelvis measured down the line at the IMP phase. A positive value indicates the pelvis has moved toward the ball/camera relative to its address position; larger positive values indicate greater thrust. The target of < 1.9" ensures the pelvis stays near its address depth rather than thrusting toward the ball.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Extension | IMP | < 1.9" | Down The Line |

## Biomechanical Significance
Early extension — the most common swing fault measured by SportsBox — occurs when the hips (and often the chest) thrust toward the ball on the downswing, forcing the golfer to stand up out of their posture through impact. When the pelvis thrusts forward by more than 1.9", it invades the space the club needs to travel through, requiring the golfer to either pull the club across the ball (causing a pull or over-the-top path) or flip the hands to find the ball with the face. Maintaining pelvis depth through impact preserves the swing arc, supports forward shaft lean, and allows the body to rotate freely past the ball.

## Fault Patterns
- **Too much thrust (> 1.9"):** Pelvis is moving toward the ball — early extension is present. This typically manifests as spine angle loss, flippy hands at impact, push-draws or pull-fades, and loss of power.
- **Within target (< 1.9"):** Pelvis is maintaining its address depth through impact; spine angle is preserved and the club can travel on a consistent arc.
