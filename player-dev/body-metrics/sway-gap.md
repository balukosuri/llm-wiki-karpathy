Measures the relative lateral offset between chest sway and pelvis sway at the top of the backswing, used to detect a reverse pivot.

## What It Measures
Calculates the difference between chest sway and pelvis sway at the TOP phase, measured face-on. A negative value indicates the chest is positioned trail-side relative to the pelvis — the correct coil pattern where the upper body stacks behind a stable lower body. A positive or near-zero value indicates the chest has shifted toward the lead side relative to the pelvis, which is the signature of a reverse pivot.

## Biomechanical Significance
Sway gap provides a combined view of how the pelvis and chest move relative to each other at the top of the backswing rather than in isolation. In a proper backswing coil, the golfer loads into the trail hip (pelvis stays relatively centered or moves slightly trail) while the chest coils behind it — resulting in a slightly negative sway gap. In a reverse pivot, the golfer's weight moves to the lead side during the backswing: the chest shifts toward the target while the pelvis may move trail-ward, creating a positive sway gap. This inverted weight distribution forces a compensatory reverse weight shift on the downswing, leading to inconsistent contact and power loss.

## Fault Patterns
- **Reverse pivot (> -0.2", approaching zero or positive):** Chest is at or ahead of the pelvis laterally — weight has shifted to the lead side in the backswing. Requires a recovery move in the downswing that leads to inconsistency.
- **Correct coil (< -0.2"):** Chest is slightly trail-side of the pelvis; upper body is loaded behind lower body, setting up an efficient weight transfer in transition.
