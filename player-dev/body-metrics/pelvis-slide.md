Measures how far the pelvis has translated toward the target during the transition phase, initiating the downswing weight shift.

## What It Measures
Tracks lateral translation of the pelvis along the target line during the transition (TRANS) phase, measured face-on. Positive values indicate the pelvis has shifted toward the lead (target) side. This metric checks that the pelvis — the base of the kinematic chain — has begun its target-ward slide before or concurrent with the end of the backswing.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shift To Lead Side Before Top | TRANS | > 0.5" | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Pelvis slide in transition is the primary initiator of the kinematic sequence on the downswing. The pelvis leading the transition by moving target-ward before the upper body and arms begin their downward motion is a hallmark of efficient, powerful swingers. This early pelvis shift creates a separation (X-factor stretch) between the hips and shoulders, loading the obliques and allowing the club to shallow from behind. When the pelvis fails to shift early, the arms and upper body initiate the downswing, producing a steep, over-the-top move.

## Fault Patterns
- **Insufficient shift (< 0.5"):** Pelvis is not moving target-ward early enough; transition is initiated by the upper body, increasing risk of steep over-the-top delivery.
- **Sufficient shift (> 0.5"):** Pelvis is leading the kinematic sequence; body is positioned to generate power from the ground up.
