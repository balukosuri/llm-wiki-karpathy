Measures the lateral translation of the chest away from or toward the target at two critical swing phases: backswing maximum and impact.

## What It Measures
Tracks the absolute lateral position of the chest segment relative to the address position, measured face-on. Negative values indicate the chest has moved toward the trail side (away from target); positive values indicate movement toward the lead (target) side.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stay Centered | BS MAX | > -2.4" | Face On |
| Get To Lead Side | IMP | > -0.3" | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Chest sway captures two distinct movement quality checks. At backswing maximum, excessive trail-side sway (beyond -2.4") indicates the golfer is swaying away from the ball rather than rotating around a stable axis, creating a reverse-weight-shift setup. At impact, the chest must be at or near neutral (> -0.3") — remaining trail-side at contact results in poor weight transfer, a rising angle of attack, and loss of power delivery to the ball.

## Fault Patterns
- **At BS MAX: too far trail (< -2.4"):** Excessive lateral sway in the backswing; body is shifting away from target rather than rotating. Often produces compensatory moves in transition and a steep, weak downswing.
- **At IMP: still trail-side (< -0.3"):** Chest has not transferred to the lead side at impact; weight is stuck on the back foot, typically causing fat or thin contact and reduced clubhead speed.
- **Within targets:** Chest is centered in the backswing and transferred forward at impact — supports efficient energy transfer through the ball.
