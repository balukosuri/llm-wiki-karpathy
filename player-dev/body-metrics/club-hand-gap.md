Measures the horizontal separation between the club head and the hands during the backswing, used to detect an inside takeaway.

## What It Measures
Quantifies the distance between the club head and the hands along the face-on axis, measured down the line during the backswing club position (BCH) phase. Negative values indicate the club head is further behind the hands relative to the target line (inside position); the more negative the value, the more the club has tracked behind the body.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Takeaway Too Inside | BCH | > -1.7" | Down The Line |

## Biomechanical Significance
The relationship between where the club head and hands travel in the takeaway largely determines the swing plane and the club's approach angle on the downswing. When the club head tracks excessively inside the hands (gap more negative than -1.7"), the club gets trapped behind the body, typically forcing an over-the-top reroute or a push/hook delivery at impact. Maintaining a proper club-hand gap ensures the club stays on plane through the first third of the backswing.

## Fault Patterns
- **Too inside (< -1.7"):** Club has tracked well inside the hands; takeaway is too flat/inside, predisposing an over-the-top reroute or blocked impact position.
- **Within target (> -1.7"):** Club and hands are tracking in appropriate proximity; takeaway plane is on or near correct path.
