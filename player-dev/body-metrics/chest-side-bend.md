Measures the lateral tilt of the chest toward the lead side at the top of the backswing, viewed down the line.

## What It Measures
Quantifies the side-bending angle of the thorax in the down-the-line view at the top of the backswing. Negative values indicate the chest is tilting toward the lead (target) side; more negative values represent a greater lead-side tilt.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat Shoulder Plane | TOP | < -35° | Down The Line |

## Biomechanical Significance
Chest side bend at the top of the backswing is the primary determinant of shoulder plane steepness. When the lead shoulder dips sufficiently (chest tilts toward lead side, < -35°), the shoulders rotate on a flatter plane, which promotes an inside-out swing path and reduces the likelihood of steep, over-the-top downswing moves. Insufficient lead-side tilt results in a steep shoulder plane and is often associated with an outside-in club path and pull/slice ball flight.

## Fault Patterns
- **Too shallow (> -35°, i.e., less than 35° of lead-side tilt):** Shoulder plane is too steep — the lead shoulder is not dipping enough, predisposing an over-the-top downswing and outside-in path.
- **Sufficient (< -35°):** Lead shoulder has dipped adequately; shoulder plane is flat enough to support an inside-out delivery.
