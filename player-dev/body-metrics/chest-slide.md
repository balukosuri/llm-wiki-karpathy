Measures how far the chest has translated toward the target (lead side) during the transition phase.

## What It Measures
Tracks lateral translation of the chest segment along the target line during the transition — the window between the end of the backswing and the beginning of the downswing. Positive values indicate lead-side movement; the target checks that the chest has shifted at least 0.6" toward the target before reaching the top of the backswing.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shift To Lead Side Before Top | TRANS | > 0.6" | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Early lateral translation of the chest toward the target in transition is a marker of proper sequencing in the kinematic chain. When the chest begins moving lead-ward before the backswing is fully complete, it initiates the weight-transfer cascade and helps shallow the club's approach angle on the downswing. Delaying this shift until after the top increases the risk of an over-the-top club path and robs the swing of ground-reaction force.

## Fault Patterns
- **Below target (< 0.6"):** The chest is not shifting lead-ward early enough; transition is driven by the arms and hands rather than the body, often producing steep, outside-in delivery.
- **At or above target (> 0.6"):** Chest is moving toward the target in transition, initiating proper weight shift and sequencing.
