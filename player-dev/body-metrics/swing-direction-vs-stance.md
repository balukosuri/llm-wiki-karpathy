Measures the club's swing direction at impact relative to the player's stance line, quantifying whether the swing path is inside-out, square, or outside-in.

## What It Measures
Captures the angular difference between the club's travel direction and the player's stance line at the IMP phase, measured down the line. Positive values indicate an inside-to-out path (club traveling to the right of stance for a right-handed golfer); negative values indicate outside-to-in. The target window of 1° to 6° represents a slightly inside-out swing direction.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside to Out | IMP | 1° to 6° | Down The Line |
| Outside to In | IMP | 1° to 6° | Down The Line |

## Biomechanical Significance
Swing direction relative to stance is the primary determinant of initial ball direction (in combination with face angle) and the shape of ball flight. A path in the 1°–6° inside-out range is considered neutral to slightly positive for most right-handed players — it promotes a draw bias and is consistent with efficient body rotation and hip clearing. Outside-in paths produce the pull/slice family of misses and are correlated with over-the-top shaft delivery (see [[shaft-angle-vs-shoulder-plane]]) and insufficient hip clearing. Both the "Inside to Out" and "Outside to In" goals converge on the same target window because both are corrections toward the same neutral zone.

## Fault Patterns
- **Too inside-out (> 6°):** Path is excessively in-to-out; club is being released from too far inside, risking push-draws, blocks, or snap hooks depending on face angle.
- **In range (1° to 6°):** Neutral to slightly inside-out — optimal for consistent, draw-biased ball flight with manageable dispersion.
- **Outside-in (< 1°, approaching or below 0°):** Path is square to outside-in; promotes pull or slice patterns. Correlates with over-the-top delivery, steep angle of attack, and insufficient hip rotation.
