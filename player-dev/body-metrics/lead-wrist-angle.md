Measures the angle at the lead wrist during the downswing at hip height, indicating how much wrist hinge (lag) is being retained.

## What It Measures
Captures the angular relationship at the lead wrist in the face-on view at the DAH (downswing at hip height) phase. A smaller angle indicates the wrist is still hinged (lag maintained); a larger angle approaching 180° means the wrist has uncocked (cast). The target < 100° indicates the hinge angle should remain below 100° when the hands are at hip height on the downswing.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop Early Casting | DAH | < 100° | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Lag — the trailing of the club head behind the hands during the downswing — is a primary mechanism for storing and releasing angular momentum into the ball. When the lead wrist angle is maintained below 100° at hip height, the club head is still trailing the hands and speed is being deferred for release through impact. Early casting (angle above 100° at DAH) dissipates this stored energy prematurely, causing a loss of clubhead speed, a steep angle of attack, and often a scooping motion through the ball.

## Fault Patterns
- **Casting (> 100°):** Wrist hinge has been released too early — lag is lost before the hands reach hip height. Typically produces a loss of speed, thin contact, or a scoop at impact. Correlates with early extension and scooping patterns.
- **Lag maintained (< 100°):** Wrist angle is preserved; club head is still trailing the hands, deferring speed release for maximum impact delivery.
