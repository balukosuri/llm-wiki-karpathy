Measures the forward lean angle of the club shaft at impact from the face-on view, indicating whether the hands lead the club head through the ball.

## What It Measures
Quantifies the angle of the club shaft relative to vertical (or the address shaft angle) at the IMP phase, measured face-on. Negative values indicate the shaft is leaning toward the target (forward shaft lean, hands-ahead position); values near zero or positive indicate the shaft is vertical or leaning away from the target (scooping). The target of < -12° requires at least 12° of forward lean at impact.

|---|---|---|---|
| Stop Scooping | IMP | < -12° | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Forward shaft lean at impact is the defining characteristic of a compressed, ball-first strike. When the hands are ahead of the club head at impact (shaft leaning toward target by more than 12°), the loft is de-lofted, contact is ball-first, and energy is efficiently transferred through the ball to the turf. Scooping — when the trail wrist extends and the hands fall behind the club head — adds loft, causes fat or thin contact, and is a direct consequence of early casting (see [[lead-wrist-angle]]). Forward lean also contributes to a descending angle of attack on irons, creating the divot-after-the-ball pattern of elite ball-strikers.

## Fault Patterns
- **Scooping (> -12°, i.e., less than 12° forward lean):** Hands are not leading the club head; shaft lean is insufficient. Results in added loft, loss of compression, fat/thin contact, and reduced distance.
- **Forward lean achieved (< -12°):** Hands are ahead of the club head at impact; ball-first contact with appropriate loft and compression.
