Measures how much the chest (thorax) rises vertically from the address position by the top of the backswing.

## What It Measures
Tracks vertical translation of the chest segment from address to the top of the backswing, measured face-on. A positive value indicates the chest has risen upward from its setup height.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maintain Posture In Backswing | TOP | < 1.1" | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Excessive chest lift (rising up) indicates a loss of forward spine angle during the backswing, commonly called "standing up." When the chest rises too much, the swing plane shifts and the golfer must compensate on the downswing — typically by re-tilting or diving toward the ball — to make contact. Maintaining spine angle through the backswing preserves the swing arc and allows consistent delivery.

## Fault Patterns
- **Too high (above 1.1"):** Spine angle is being lost — the golfer is rising out of their address posture. This can cause thin contact, inconsistent ball-striking, and compensatory moves on the downswing.
- **Within target (< 1.1"):** Chest remains at or close to address height, indicating stable spine angle through the backswing.
