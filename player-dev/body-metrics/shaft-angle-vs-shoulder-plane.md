Measures the angle of the club shaft relative to the shoulder plane during the downswing, detecting over-the-top or under-plane shaft delivery.

## What It Measures
Quantifies the angular relationship between the club shaft and the shoulder plane in the down-the-line view at the DCV (mid-downswing) phase. Negative values indicate the shaft is tracking below the shoulder plane (shallower); values near zero or positive indicate the shaft is at or above the shoulder plane (steeper). The target window of -15° to -9° defines the range where the shaft is below the shoulder plane by an appropriate amount.

## Biomechanical Significance
The relationship between the shaft angle and the shoulder plane is one of the clearest indicators of swing plane efficiency on the downswing. When the shaft tracks 9°–15° below the shoulder plane, the club is on an inside, shallow approach that promotes a square-to-slightly-in-to-out path through impact. If the shaft is above the shoulder plane (less negative than -9° or positive), it is "over the top" — leading to an outside-in swing path, steep angle of attack, and the characteristic pull/slice pattern. If the shaft is too far below the shoulder plane (more negative than -15°), the club is excessively trapped and may produce push-draws or snap hooks.

## Fault Patterns
- **Over the top (> -9°, i.e., shaft at or above shoulder plane):** Club is tracking above the shoulder plane in the downswing; delivers outside-in, causing pull or slice.
- **In range (-15° to -9°):** Shaft is below shoulder plane by the right amount; inside attack angle supports on-plane, inside-out delivery.
- **Too shallow (< -15°):** Shaft is extremely below the shoulder plane; club is trapped too far inside, risking a push or snap hook if the face is not corrected.
