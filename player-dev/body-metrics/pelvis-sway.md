Measures the lateral position of the pelvis relative to address at backswing maximum and impact, tracking both the backswing centering and the impact weight shift.

## What It Measures
Tracks lateral translation of the pelvis along the target line at two phases, measured face-on. Negative values indicate the pelvis has moved toward the trail side (away from target); positive values indicate movement toward the lead side. Three separate goal windows are evaluated: one at backswing maximum and two at impact (a minimum and a maximum to bound the range).

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stay Centered | BS MAX | > -1.7" | Face On |
| Get To Lead Side | IMP | > 2.7" | Face On |
| Reduce Slide | IMP | < 4.7" | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Pelvis sway is the pelvis-level analog to chest sway and provides a foundation reading for weight distribution across all three checkpoints. At backswing maximum, excessive trail-side sway (< -1.7") indicates the pelvis is sliding away from the target rather than rotating — destabilizing the base and creating timing issues in transition. At impact, the pelvis must have moved at least 2.7" lead-ward, reflecting proper weight transfer and the lateral drive that produces ground-reaction force; however, sliding beyond 4.7" constitutes excessive lateral slide that inhibits rotation and forces a weak, hands-dominated delivery.

## Fault Patterns
- **At BS MAX: excessive sway (< -1.7"):** Pelvis is sliding away from target in the backswing rather than rotating; this robs stability, disrupts timing, and often triggers a reverse pivot.
- **At IMP: insufficient transfer (< 2.7"):** Weight has not adequately shifted to the lead side by impact; ball contact will suffer from reduced ground force and poor sequencing.
- **At IMP: excessive slide (> 4.7"):** Pelvis has slid too far lead-ward without rotating; hips are blocking out and failing to clear, typically forcing a flip or an over-the-top rescue with the upper body.
