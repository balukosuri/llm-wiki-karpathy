Measures the rotational angle of the chest around the vertical axis at address, backswing maximum, and impact.

## What It Measures
Tracks chest rotation (thorax turn) in the face-on view across three swing phases. Negative values indicate the chest is turned away from the target (backswing direction); positive values indicate it is open toward the target. This metric is evaluated at address, the full backswing, and at impact.

## Target Windows by Goal
| Goal | Phase | Target | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alignment | ADR | < 10° | Face On |
| Make A Full Turn | BS MAX | < -92° | Face On |
| Get Body More Open | IMP | > 23° | Face On |

## Biomechanical Significance
Chest turn drives power generation and swing direction across three key checkpoints. At address, a square chest (< 10° open) establishes a neutral starting position for consistent alignment. At backswing maximum, a full chest turn of at least 92° creates thoracic coil and stores elastic energy in the trunk. At impact, a chest that has rotated back past 23° open to the target indicates the body is driving the downswing and not "blocking" — enabling proper sequencing and clubhead release.

## Fault Patterns
- **At ADR: too open (> 10°):** Chest is aimed left (for right-handers) at setup; likely to produce an outside-in path or pull-oriented ball flight.
- **At BS MAX: insufficient turn (> -92°, i.e., less than 92° of turn):** Incomplete backswing rotation; limits power potential and often leads to an arms-dominated, steep downswing.
- **At IMP: not open enough (< 23°):** Chest is still facing too far away from target at contact; body is lagging behind the club, often causing flipping of the hands or blocked shots.
