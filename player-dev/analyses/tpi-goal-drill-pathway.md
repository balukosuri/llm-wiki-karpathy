Cross-reference map showing the full pathway from TPI screen failure → linked SportsBox goals → corrective exercises → technical swing drills, enabling practitioners to build integrated player development programs that sequence physical preparation before technical training.

## How to Use This Table
Read each row left to right:

1. **TPI Screen** — The physical assessment that failed or showed restriction. This is the entry point: a documented screen failure determines which goals are at risk and which interventions are indicated.
2. **Linked SB Goals** — The SportsBox improvement objectives most dependent on the physical quality tested by that screen. A restriction here does not guarantee the fault is present, but significantly raises the probability.
3. **Key Corrective Exercises** — Body preparation exercises that address the movement limitation. These are programmed first, in the activation or movement prep block, to create the physical capacity needed for the drill to be achievable.
4. **Technical Drills** — Swing technique drills that train the movement pattern once the physical restriction is reduced. Drills attempted before the physical limitation is addressed will often create compensatory patterns or fail to produce lasting change.

A player with a documented [[pelvic-tilt]] failure, for example, should receive the corrective exercises (Hip Hinge, Bridge Palms Down, T-Spine Extension) before being placed on the [[power-cord-drill]] or [[sweep-the-dust]] drill. Running the drill first without the physical prep is a common cause of stalled technical progress.

## Sequencing Protocol
The pathway has three phases that must occur in order for lasting improvement:

**Phase 1 — Physical Preparation (exercises)**
Address the movement limitation through corrective and activation exercises programmed in the warm-up and accessory blocks. The goal is restoring the physical capacity (mobility, stability, or strength) that the TPI screen identified as deficient. Re-screen periodically to confirm improvement before advancing.

**Phase 2 — Technical Training (drills)**
Once the physical restriction is reduced, introduce the corresponding technical drills. The player now has the physical capacity to execute the drill without compensating. Drill volume and frequency should follow the technical drill prescriptions on each linked drill page (typically 3–5× per week at the start of a practice session before ball-striking fatigue sets in).

**Phase 3 — SportsBox Re-Measurement**
After 4–8 weeks of consistent drill practice with the physical prep in place, re-measure the linked SportsBox goals. Movement change in the targeted body metric (e.g. higher PELVIS_TURN at impact for [[impact-more-open-at-impact]], reduced PELVIS_SLIDE for [[impact-reduce-slide]]) confirms the pathway is working. If metrics have not shifted, revisit whether the screen restriction is fully resolved or whether the drill is being executed correctly.

**Common error:** Practitioners frequently skip Phase 1 and jump directly to drills. When the physical limitation is still present, drills produce temporary improvement at best and entrench compensations at worst. The screen result is the gate — do not advance to drills until the screen moves from Fail toward Pass.
