67 squat, hinge, and lower body accessory exercises that build the strength, power, and ground-force capacity required for efficient weight transfer and rotational speed in the golf swing.

## What This Category Trains
The squat and hinge patterns develop lower body strength and power from the ground up. Squats build bilateral and unilateral leg strength, stability under load, and the ability to decelerate and accelerate through the trail-to-lead weight shift. Hinges develop posterior-chain power (glutes, hamstrings) and the hip separation mechanics that underpin rotational speed generation. Together these patterns address the physical substrate for every major TPI lower body limitation and directly support pelvic drive and lead-side bracing through the downswing and impact.

## TPI Screens Addressed
Lower body strength exercises target restrictions identified in:
- [[overhead-deep-squat]] — ankle, hip, and thoracic mobility under loaded squat demand
- [[deep-squat]] — hip flexion and ankle dorsiflexion capacity
- [[toe-touch]] — hamstring length and lumbopelvic hinge control
- [[single-leg-balance]] — unilateral stability and hip abductor strength
- [[bridge-glute-max]] — glute max activation and hip extension strength
- [[bridge-leg-extension]] — posterior chain integration across hip and knee
- [[pelvic-rotation]] — hip internal and external rotation under load
- [[lower-quarter-rotation]] — lower limb rotational range of motion
- [[torso-rotation]] — thoracic and lumbopelvic mobility in loaded positions
- [[lat-test]] — overhead mobility affecting squat pattern quality and backswing arc
- [[seated-trunk-rotation]] — thoracic rotation in stable base positions

## Golf Application
A restricted or weak lower body limits the golfer's ability to create a stable base, generate ground reaction force, and transfer energy from the ground through the pelvis into the thorax. Squat-pattern strength underpins the trail-leg loading in the backswing and the explosive lead-leg bracing through impact — directly supporting [[downswing-get-to-lead-side]] and [[impact-reduce-slide]]. Hinge-pattern power drives pelvic thrust and the rapid posterior-chain extension sequence in the downswing, supporting [[backswing-full-turn]] and [[impact-more-open-at-impact]]. Unilateral variants (split squats, single-leg deadlifts) directly mirror the asymmetric weight-bearing demands of the golf swing. Rotational jump and lunge variants (90° box jumps, rotational lunges) bridge the gap between isolated lower body strength and golf-specific explosive rotation.

## Programming Notes
**Activation block:** Place Activation-category exercises in warm-up or movement prep (2–3 sets × 8–12 reps). Prioritize exercises with TPI screen linkage when the player has a documented failure on the linked screen — these directly address the physical limitation before loading the main pattern.

**Main strength block:** Load bilateral squats (Barbell Back/Front Squat, Hex Bar Deadlift) and bilateral hinges (Barbell Deadlift, Romanian Deadlift) at 3–5 sets × 3–8 reps for strength development. Unilateral variants (Bulgarian Split Squat, Single Leg RDL) complement bilateral work and address the lateral strength asymmetries common in golfers.

**Power block:** Perform explosive exercises (Box Jump, 90 Degree Box Jump, Squat Jump, Golf Jumps, KB Swing, Barbell Hang Clean, DB Snatch, 2 DB Hang Power Clean) before fatigue accumulates from strength work. Use 3–5 sets × 3–5 reps at maximal intent. Rotational jumps (90 Degree Box Jump, Broad Jump, Squat Jump) are highest specificity — programme late in a mesocycle when the base strength is established.

**Rotation integration:** Exercises combining squat/hinge with rotation (KB Single Arm Racked Rotational Reverse Lunge, Alternating Reverse Lunge + RB Torso Rotation, MB Bear Hug Stepback Hip Hinge + Torso Rotation) bridge isolated strength and swing-specific movement. Programme after the main strength block, 2–3 sets × 6–10 reps each side.

**Screen-driven prioritization:** When a player fails [[overhead-deep-squat]], [[single-leg-balance]], or [[bridge-leg-extension]], promote corresponding Activation exercises to session priority until screen performance improves on re-assessment.
