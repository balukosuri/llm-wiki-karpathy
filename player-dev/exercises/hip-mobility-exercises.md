Hip mobility exercise library targeting hip joint range of motion, adductor/posterior chain flexibility, and pelvis-femur dissociation to support efficient rotational sequencing in the golf swing.
## What This Category Trains
Hip mobility exercises develop the active and passive range of motion required for the pelvis to rotate freely over the femur during the backswing and through-swing. Restricted hip mobility forces compensations such as early extension, sway, or slide as the body seeks rotation from proximate segments. Sufficient hip mobility is a prerequisite for pelvic-torso dissociation, which is central to building lag and generating ground-force leverage.

## SportsBox Goals Supported
- [[impact-more-open-at-impact]] — pelvic rotation at impact requires adequate hip internal rotation range on the lead side
- [[backswing-full-turn]] — trail hip external rotation mobility enables a full pelvis turn away from the ball
- [[backswing-stay-centered]] — hip mobility allows rotation without lateral sway
- [[impact-early-extension]] — restricted hip mobility is a primary driver of early extension; these exercises address root cause
- [[impact-reduce-slide]] — adequate hip mobility allows the pelvis to rotate rather than slide toward the target

## How to Use This Library
Hip mobility work belongs in the **prep block** at the start of every session. Begin with 2–3 foam roll exercises (60–90 seconds per site) to reduce tissue density, then progress to passive stretches, and finish with active mobility drills (CARS, shin box flows, golf-posture rotations) that integrate the range of motion into movement patterns. Driver/dowel exercises at the end of the prep block bridge hip mobility directly into golf-specific posture and movement, making them an effective transition into technical work. For players with significant pelvic rotation restrictions, prioritize 90/90 Shin Box variants and Hip Flexor Mobility flows 3–5 times per week until screen improvements are confirmed.
