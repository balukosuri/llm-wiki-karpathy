Neck mobility and wrist/forearm mobility exercise library addressing cervical rotation range and distal arm complex mobility to support consistent head position, grip mechanics, and wrist hinge control throughout the golf swing.
## What This Category Trains
**Neck Mobility** exercises restore cervical rotation and extension range to allow the head to remain relatively stable while the thorax rotates around it during the backswing, and to track the ball through impact and follow-through without restriction. Cervical rotation limitations can force excessive trunk movement to compensate, disrupting swing plane and posture. **Wrist and Forearm Mobility** exercises address the multi-planar demands of the grip and clubface control — specifically flexion/extension range for hinge depth, and forearm pronation/supination for face angle management during the swing. Restrictions in these segments contribute to casting, scooping, and compensatory grip adjustments that reduce consistency.

## How to Use This Library
Neck mobility exercises are best placed at the start of the **prep block**, requiring no equipment and minimal space. Two to three sets of 8–10 slow repetitions per direction (supine or prone) are sufficient to restore baseline cervical range before a session. Wrist and forearm mobility exercises belong in the **prep block** immediately before any technical or range work where grip mechanics and wrist position are emphasized. Quadruped wrist variants (bodyweight) precede loaded forearm rotation (dumbbell) in the progression — resolve any wrist discomfort in the bodyweight positions before adding load. The bench-supported DB supination and pronation exercises can also serve as isolated corrective homework performed daily for players with a forearm rotation screen failure. Frequency of 5–7 days per week is appropriate for this distal segment work given the low fatigue cost.
