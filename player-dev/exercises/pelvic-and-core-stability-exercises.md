Pelvic and core stability exercise library training anterior/posterior pelvic control, global core stiffness, and rotational anti-movement capacity to maintain posture and transfer power efficiently through the golf swing.
## What This Category Trains
**Pelvic Stability** exercises develop the ability to control anterior and posterior pelvic tilt under load and during rotation — the foundation of S-posture management, C-posture reduction, and sway/slide prevention. **Core Stability** exercises train the full cylinder of trunk musculature (rectus abdominis, obliques, transverse abdominis, erector spinae) in both anti-flexion and anti-rotation tasks. Together, these categories provide the rigid link between the lower and upper body that allows rotational force generated at the hips and thorax to be efficiently transferred to the club without energy leakage through the lumbar spine.

## How to Use This Library
Pelvic stability exercises are best used as **activation work** in the prep block, specifically the supine and golf-stance pelvic tilt drills, which re-establish positional awareness before loading. Mini band clamshell and glute bridge progressions can serve as warm-up activation or standalone corrective sessions. Plank and core stability exercises belong in the **core/power block** at the end of the main session, after strength work, to train the stiffened trunk under fatigue conditions that better replicate late-swing demands. The 1/2 kneeling cable series (chops, lifts, Pallof) trains rotational core stiffness in postures that directly simulate the body positions encountered in the downswing and impact — these are particularly high-value for players with slide or early extension tendencies. Program 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps for chop/lift variations, and 20–30 second holds for anti-rotation press work.
