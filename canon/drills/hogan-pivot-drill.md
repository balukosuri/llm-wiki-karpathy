Take the club halfway back, pause, then fire the hips first — the most direct method for training the hip-led downswing sequence that Hogan identified as the primary driver of power and accuracy.

## Purpose

The Hogan Pivot Drill addresses downswing initiation — the most critical and most commonly failed moment in the golf swing. At the top of the backswing, the vast majority of amateur golfers begin the downswing with the arms or hands, throwing the club outward and destroying both sequencing and plane before the ball is reached.

Hogan's system requires the opposite: the **hips fire first**, initiating a kinetic chain from the ground up. The weight shifts to the lead foot. The torso follows the hips. The arms descend as a consequence of the body's rotation, not as independent throwers. The club is delivered last.

The drill isolates this moment by removing the full backswing and giving the player a clean, unhurried starting position — club at halfway back, belt high — from which the only question is: what moves first?

## Procedure

1. Take the club halfway back to belt height and pause. The club should be roughly parallel to the ground, pointing along the target line or just inside it, with the upper body coiled against the lower body's resistance.
2. From this paused position, initiate the downswing by firing the hips forward first. Feel the weight begin to shift from the back foot to the lead foot. The hips move toward the target before the arms move.
3. Allow the arms and club to follow naturally as the hips and torso unwind. Do not throw or cast the club outward. The arms are passengers responding to the body's rotation — they should feel like they are being pulled down rather than thrown.
4. Finish balanced on the lead foot with a full, relaxed finish position.

## Feel Cue

> "Swing through the ball with your trailing butt pocket."

This is Hogan's most specific downswing cue. The trailing butt pocket (the right glute for a right-handed player) leads the motion toward the target. When the trailing butt pocket fires, the hips have begun to rotate correctly. The mental image places the power source in the hips and glutes rather than the arms — which is exactly where Hogan's power lived.

The secondary feel: the arms feel late. This is correct. In a properly sequenced downswing, the arms lag behind the hips and torso at the start of the downswing, creating lag in the club and the lag-to-release pattern that produces both power and compression.

## Common Errors

**Starting the downswing with the arms or hands.** The primary fault. The player reaches the paused halfway-back position and immediately throws the arms downward, bypassing the hip initiation entirely. The result is an over-the-top move, an outside-in path, and a loss of the lag that Hogan's system creates through correct sequencing. Correction: exaggerate the hip movement. Make the hips move so obviously forward that it feels like the arms are not moving at all in the first instant of the downswing.

**Lack of hip rotation.** A related fault: the hips shift laterally toward the target (slide) without rotating. A lateral slide moves weight forward but does not create the rotational platform from which the arms and club can be delivered on plane. The hips must both shift and rotate — not one or the other. The feel cue "trailing butt pocket" specifically targets rotation rather than slide: a butt pocket fires rotationally, not laterally.

**Pausing too long at halfway back.** The drill requires a momentary pause, not a prolonged stop. A pause long enough to completely arrest the swing's momentum can create an artificial, lurching restart. The pause should be just enough to reset — two to three seconds — then fire.

## Best Use

Use the Hogan Pivot Drill:

- As the primary drill for players struggling with downswing initiation — the standard diagnostic is a pattern of pulls, slices, and over-the-top shots
- For players who understand the hip-led sequence intellectually but cannot feel it in a full swing — the halfway-back pause eliminates the backswing complexity and reduces the problem to a single question
- As a warm-up drill before full-swing practice sessions to prime the sequencing feel
- For mid-handicap to competitive players: the dossier prescribes this as a "Bogey Golfer" starting drill but notes it runs through the competitive level for players with transition issues

The dossier's practice system prescribes a dedicated "Pivot and Downswing Sequence" block of 30 minutes, combining the Hogan Pivot Drill with weight-shift drills. Success measure: hips initiate downswing; balanced finish.

## Progression

1. **Drill only (no ball).** Perform the full procedure — halfway back, pause, fire hips, finish — without a ball. Feel each component of the sequence clearly before introducing the pressure of contact.
2. **Add ball contact — short irons.** Introduce a ball with a short iron. The short iron's steep attack angle is more forgiving of sequencing hesitation; errors are instructive without being destructive.
3. **Progress to mid-irons and woods.** As the hip-led feel becomes automatic, extend to longer clubs. The longer the club, the more the correct sequence is rewarded with additional distance.
4. **Full swings — varied clubs.** Practice the Pivot Drill concept across all clubs in a practice session. The goal: the hip initiation is the automatic starting move on every downswing, not a consciously triggered event.
