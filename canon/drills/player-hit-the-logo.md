Player's precision putting drill: orient the ball's logo toward the putter face and strike it squarely before looking up. The logo gives the eyes a small, specific target through impact — more actionable than "keep your head down" because it tells the eyes exactly where to look and what to confirm.

## Procedure

1. Place the golf ball so the logo faces directly toward the putter face. The logo should be perpendicular to the ground and aimed squarely at the putter.
2. Set up with eyes over the ball, using your normal putting stance and grip.
3. Make a smooth putting stroke, focusing entirely on striking the logo squarely with the putter face. The only task at impact is logo contact — centered, direct, and clean.
4. Observe the ball's roll after impact. A square strike on the logo produces end-over-end roll (true overspin) with no side wobble. Off-center contact produces a wobbling or skidding ball that self-diagnoses the error.
5. Adjust the stroke based on roll feedback. Repeat until the roll is consistently true.

## Feel Cue

> "Strike the logo dead center." — Gary Player

This is the operative thought at impact. Not "keep your head down" — that is a constraint without a positive target. "Strike the logo dead center" is a target instruction that produces head stability as a byproduct. The eyes stay on the logo because they need to see the putter arrive at it.

A companion cue: *"Don't peek — hold your gaze."* The head lifts early because the brain wants to see where the ball went. The logo gives the eyes an alternative task: confirm that the putter struck it squarely. This delays the head lift until well after the putter has passed impact.

## Why This Is More Precise Than "Keep Your Head Down"

"Keep your head down" is a constraint. It tells the player what not to do but provides no positive instruction about where to direct attention. The result is often tension — the player holding the head artificially still through willpower, which introduces rigidity into the stroke.

"Strike the logo" replaces the constraint with a task. The eyes have a specific, small target to track through impact. Head stability becomes a consequence of task completion, not a separately managed discipline. Players who adopt this drill report the head stays naturally because the eyes are still busy — watching the logo, confirming the strike, reading the initial roll direction.

The additional diagnostic value is significant. A player who believes they are hitting solid putts but is actually missing the sweet spot will see the ball roll poorly — wobbly, skidding, inconsistent — and immediately understand why. The logo turns every practice putt into a contact audit that is both visible and unambiguous.

## Common Errors

**Early head lift causing thin or fat contact.** The most frequent fault. The player looks up before or during impact to watch the ball, and the putter head rises out of the stroke plane. The result is either a thin strike — the blade catches the top of the ball — or, if the player compensates by dropping, a fat contact below the equator. The drill directly corrects this: hold gaze on the logo until the roll begins.

**Orienting the logo incorrectly.** The ball must be placed with the logo perpendicular to the putting line and facing the putter face. A logo oriented to the sides or facing the sky provides no useful contact feedback. Check the setup deliberately before each practice putt.

**Focusing on the outcome rather than the contact.** Beginners to this drill watch to see if the putt goes in the hole rather than whether the logo contact was square. This defeats the drill's purpose. Direct all attention to contact quality first. Accurate, reliable rolls — and made putts — will follow from centered strikes.

## Best Use

The Hit the Logo Drill is most effective for:

- Any handicap level experiencing inconsistent contact on short putts (0–6 feet)
- Players who struggle with early head lift — the most common amateur putting fault
- Pre-round warmup to calibrate contact feel before a competitive round
- Pairing with Player's "Feel the Distance" lag-putting work: use Hit the Logo for short-range precision; use Feel the Distance for longer-range touch and speed control

Player used this drill as part of a complete putting system alongside "Keep Head Down Through Impact," "Study the Grain," and "Feel the Distance." The logo drill is the contact-precision anchor for that system.

## Progression

**Starting level:** 3-foot putts with the logo correctly oriented. Stroke, hold gaze, observe roll. Make 10 consecutive putts with true roll before extending the distance.

**Intermediate:** Extend to 6 feet. Maintain logo-contact focus throughout. Success metric: 75% make rate with consistently true roll on every putt, made or missed.

**Advanced pressure:** Count consecutive solid-contact putts. Add a consequence for breaking focus — start the count over from zero. This simulates competitive pressure and tests whether the logo-focus holds when something is at stake, reproducing the condition Player faced when putting to win Majors.
