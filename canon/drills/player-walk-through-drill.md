Player's signature weight-transfer training drill: step forward with the trail foot as the club passes impact, forcing full weight commitment to the lead side and eliminating the "hang back" fault that robs power and produces inconsistent contact.

## Procedure

1. Address a mid-iron ball with a normal stance and setup.
2. Take a slow practice swing. As the club passes the impact position, step forward with the trail foot — moving it toward the target as the clubhead releases through the hitting area.
3. Feel the body's weight shift completely onto the lead foot. The trail foot should land near the lead foot, confirming that momentum carried the body through, not stopped at impact.
4. Hit actual balls, allowing the trail foot's forward step to happen naturally as a result of swing momentum. Do not force it mechanically, but do not prevent it either.
5. Repeat, focusing on balance and rhythm throughout the motion, not on the step itself.

## Feel Cue

> "Step through like you're walking the dog." — Gary Player

The image is exact: when walking a dog on a leash, forward momentum is continuous and natural. The trailing leg comes forward because the body is moving. Apply the same logic to the swing. The trail foot does not stay planted because staying planted would mean the body stopped. Let the momentum walk you through.

A secondary cue from Player's instruction: *"Transfer all energy into and through the hitting area."* The step is the external confirmation that energy was transferred, not absorbed. If the step doesn't happen, the energy stayed in the body.

## Common Errors

**Hanging back on the trail foot.** The most common fault this drill corrects. A player who hangs back shifts weight only partially — the trail hip stays behind, the lead hip stalls, and the hands compensate by firing early. Contact becomes inconsistent. The trail foot step will not happen naturally until the weight transfer is complete; if the foot wants to stay planted, the weight stayed on the wrong side.

**Early extension.** Standing up out of posture through impact to allow the forward step, rather than maintaining spine angle and letting the step happen from momentum. The step should be driven by swing momentum, not by the player lunging toward the target. Keep the posture angles through impact; the step follows as a consequence.

**Forcing the step mechanically.** Stepping forward deliberately as a conscious action without completing the swing is not the drill. The step must be a consequence of full swing momentum. Use slow practice swings first to build the feeling, then transfer to actual ball-striking where the step happens because the swing demands it.

## Best Use

The Walk-Through Drill is most effective for:

- Players who hang back on the trail side through impact — the most common fault in those who try to "scoop" or "help" the ball into the air
- Players losing swing speed without an obvious mechanical cause (incomplete weight transfer is a primary speed leak)
- Players seeking more consistent shot shape and trajectory through improved lead-hip rotation
- Mid-range and higher handicappers learning to trust body-driven mechanics over hand-dependent saves

The drill directly trains the same mechanical sequence that Player used in competition: aggressive weight transfer initiating the downswing, lead hip clearing to square the face, and full energy delivery through the ball. It was a central part of the preparation that powered his final-round 64 at the 1978 Masters — seven birdies from seven shots back, executed with the same physical mechanics this drill installs.

## Progression

**Starting level:** Mid-iron (7-iron preferred), slow practice swings with deliberate step-through. Focus on the feel of the trail foot landing after impact, not on ball contact.

**Intermediate:** Hit actual balls with the mid-iron, allowing the step-through to happen naturally. Evaluate contact and ball flight — solid contact with consistent trajectory confirms the weight transfer is working.

**Advanced:** Progress to full swings with driver. The step-through will be less exaggerated with the driver due to wider stance, but the sensation of weight moving through impact should be the same. Add video feedback to monitor lead-hip rotation angle and trail-foot position at finish.

**Competition readiness:** Use as a warmup drill before a round to activate the weight-transfer pattern. The kinetic memory of the step-through carries into regular swings with a normal finish.
