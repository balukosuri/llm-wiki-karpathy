One belt above the elbows, one above the knees — slow rehearsal swings with both belts in place make arms-body separation immediately detectable and force the connected, structurally sound motion that defines Hogan's Two-Belt principle.

## Purpose

The Two-Belt Drill trains the fundamental of arms-body connection — Hogan's requirement that the torso and arms move as a single integrated unit rather than independently. When the arms lift, cast, or move ahead of or behind the body's rotation, the swing loses structural integrity: the hands must compensate, producing inconsistency and the handsy motion that Hogan identified as the most common swing-destroying fault.

The belts are physical constraints that make the correct feeling unmistakable. The player cannot escape into arm-only motion while the belt above the elbows constrains the arms to the body. The constraint produces the correct sensation; removing the belts requires the player to voluntarily reproduce the feeling without external enforcement.

This drill is the primary prescription for golfers with disconnected or handsy swings, and it is the concrete method through which the Two-Belt principle described in [[hogan-fundamentals-system]] is trained.

## Procedure

1. Wrap one belt snugly just above the elbows, connecting the upper arms to the torso. The belt should be firm enough that any separation of the elbows from the body creates clear tension on the belt.
2. Wrap a second belt just above the knees. This belt reinforces the lower-body connection and prevents the legs from working independently of the pivot.
3. Make slow, deliberate rehearsal swings without a ball. Focus entirely on moving the arms and body as a single unit. At this pace, the player can feel whether the arms are separating from the torso at any point in the swing, because the upper belt will immediately signal the fault.
4. Remove both belts. Make short swings — half-swings to three-quarter swings — attempting to replicate the connected feeling without the physical constraint. The sensation from the belted swings is the target.
5. Gradually progress to full swings while maintaining the connected feeling. The arms and body should feel as though they are driven by the same rotational force.

## Feel Cue

> "Feel your arms and torso move as one piece."

The sensation to seek: there is no independent arm movement. The arms are carried by the body's rotation, not swung separately. On the backswing, the left arm stays connected to the chest (for a right-handed player) as the torso turns; on the downswing, the arms descend as the hips and torso unwind — not by throwing the arms independently.

A related cue from the dossier: **"Training Connection" — arms and body move as a unit, not independently.** This is both the instruction and the diagnostic. If the arms feel like they are doing the work, the connection has broken.

## Common Errors

**Arms moving independently of the body.** The most common fault this drill addresses. On the backswing, the arms lift or the left arm separates from the chest. On the downswing, the arms throw or cast outward rather than descending with the body. Both produce a swing that cannot be repeated. With the upper belt in place, the separation is instantly detectable.

**Hands dominating the swing.** A secondary manifestation of disconnection: the hands and wrists become the primary movers, with the body following rather than leading. This produces a flipping or scooping motion at impact and removes the structural integrity that makes consistent, compressing contact possible. The correction is to feel the body turn first and carry the hands as passengers. The belt drill makes this unmistakable at slow speed.

**Too much tension in the belt constraint.** Wrapping the belt too tightly creates artificial stiffness and prevents the arms from moving within their correct range. The belt should be snug enough to signal separation but loose enough that a connected swing feels free and natural.

## Best Use

Use the Two-Belt Drill:

- As the primary drill for any golfer diagnosed with a disconnected or handsy swing
- In dedicated drill blocks during practice sessions — the dossier prescribes 20 minutes of connected-rhythm work as a dedicated practice block
- Before full-swing ball striking sessions to establish the connected feeling before adding ball contact
- When ball-striking becomes inconsistent and the pattern suggests arms racing ahead of or falling behind the body

The dossier's practice system prescribes this drill as part of a dedicated "Connection Drills (Two-Belt)" block: 20 minutes of belted rehearsal swings followed by short and full swings without belts, maintaining the feeling. Success measure: arms and body move in unison; reduced handsy motion.

## Progression

1. **Belted slow-motion swings (no ball).** Establish the connected sensation. Take as many sessions as necessary at this stage — the feeling must be unmistakable before moving forward.
2. **Belt-free short swings (no ball).** Remove belts. Make half-swings to three-quarter swings replicating the connected feeling without physical enforcement.
3. **Belt-free full swings (no ball).** Progress to full swings. The arms should feel carried, not driven.
4. **Add ball contact — short irons.** Introduce a ball with short irons at moderate tempo. Connection is more fragile under ball-contact pressure; return to slow-motion swings if disconnection creeps back.
5. **Increase swing speed.** Gradually add speed while maintaining connection. Monitor for the arms racing ahead at higher speeds — this is the most common regression point.
